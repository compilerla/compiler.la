Last week I had the pleasure of attending Transport Ticketing North America for the first time. I have to say as someone who attends a lot of transportation conferences every year: This is definitely my new favorite national conference in the transportation space. Here’s what I think are the big takeaways, key themes, and some things I’d like to see happen next.

## The big takeaways I left with

### Full-fare single tickets for adults are done

Broadly, agencies have completely implemented a contactless open-loop payment that works for this market segment. For many, this focus was driven by one or both of the following: a goal to capture a desired market segment — visitors, tourists, or single-trip riders — and an ideal place to test a new technology. Next up, agencies are looking at new goal posts around lowering the costs of collecting fares and improving the experience of paying for transit for all riders.

### The time to work on concessions or discounted-fares is now

In the last five years, the industry has solved almost every known problem around implementing contactless open-loop payments for a single, full-fare ride. California has built — and continues to develop — its flagship agile open source software product, [Cal-ITP Benefits](https://compiler.la/blog/2026/launching-cal-itp-benefits/). Today it supports older adults, Medicare card holders, veterans, and low-income verification using SNAP benefits. _Stay tuned for big news coming soon from our team\!_

Other pilot programs are exploring solutions for more complex eligibility situations, like [people with disabilities](https://compiler.la/blog/2026/reduced-fare-eligibility-research/), college students, employer programs, and funding integrations with new payment providers. Digital identity tools power almost all of these pilots today. However, digital identity is rapidly evolving, and while it’s not sufficient today, there are opportunities to launch limited-scope pilots that will lay the groundwork for what’s going to be commonplace in five years.

### Cash handling continues to be expensive across agencies regardless of size.

While different agencies have different plans for the future of cash, there’s still a general trend to get cash point of sale off of vehicles, and even out of stations entirely. Some agencies are consolidating locations for expensive to operate cash fare machines or dramatically expanding retail partnerships. Toronto’s goal for their retail network measures walking time for a user to access a retail network location from anywhere in Toronto.

### Calculate your total cost to collect fares

Costs to operate closed-loop systems have been a key driver in agencies moving towards open-loop payments, but until recently it was rare for an agency to calculate these costs independently of other operating costs. Several agencies shared rough numbers and estimated cost savings compared to new open-loop systems that have been deployed both per vehicle and systemwide. For many having this new cost — especially broken down by payment types and fare products – will help inform priorities for future phases as they look at incentivizing riders to choose lower cost to deliver fare products.

### All the cool kids are offering incentives

Monterey Salinas Transit, whose geography makes cash collection logistically impossible, has a timeline to take cash off of its vehicles in 2027\. Today, their fares offer the best bang for your buck to riders choosing to tap to pay and hitting a daily, weekly, or monthly fare cap. Santa Cruz METRO has a daily fare cap that is \$1 cheaper if you use an open loop card. How to make sure incentives are equitable? This is a question RTD is tackling by modeling novel ideas, one of which includes increasing single ride tickets but keeping the monthly pass flat.

### Simplifying your fares is sage advice

Over and over we’ve heard from agencies that both did and didn’t simplify fare policies before implementing new fare technologies. Both had come to the same conclusions: Fare simplification is a win for agencies, a win for vendors, and a win for riders. In some cases, **simplification also means standardization** across a region, which allows agencies to take advantage of and leverage buying power for bulk discounts.

Examples included the state of California’s Mobility Marketplace, and its prenegotiated MSAs for payment hardware and software, as well as the GoCard collaborative procurement led by MST, which holds that as long as collaborating agencies agree to use the same card design, they can cooperatively take advantage of more discounts. Santa Cruz METRO changed its fare policy for their senior program, raising it from 62+ to 65+ years of age. This way, they could take advantage of the tool Caltrans funded to digitize self-service eligibility for senior programs using the same definition as the California Department of Motor Vehicles.

### Define Debt recovery

ORCA knows what’s up. Paul Kulchenko, director of product delivery at Sound Transit, gave a great presentation explaining what debt recovery means for transit operators. He also outlined key vocabulary that was new to much of the audience who are earlier in their journey, and he shared ORCA’s current policies for debt recovery. The policy has evolved to create today’s structure that balances card processor-preferred merchant behavior, manages costs to capture debts, and improves the experience for the customer. There were also, of course, absolutely fantastic charts. If you’re a transit agency – it’s definitely a deck your team wants to get its hands on.

## Other things of note

### Fare enforcement was mostly referenced in a sort of past tense

Major improvements in recent years were mentioned by more than one agency. Instead of beefing up fare enforcement, these agencies are now looking to rider messaging and marketing campaigns focused on changing behavior. For example, one message might be: Pay your fare and we can run better service. It gives people an incentive rather than a punishment.

### Consistency is key

Providing a consistent experience for your rides – and per Transport for London that means consistent across industries – can mean less demand or burden on your vehicle operators and customer service staff. Becoming a merchant means offloading some things to the customer’s bank instead of your own customer service and finance teams. This can mean faster adoption for tourists who don’t speak the language you operate in.

### The future is still uncertain

We don’t know what the future holds – and it’s essential we hold that truth when we scope technology procurements. Procurements must be designed to adapt with the market over the next ten years instead of guessing or assuming what it might look like. This isn’t a new idea, but it’s one worth repeating and shouting from the rooftops until it becomes industry standard

## Key themes this year

### Friction

Executives recommend that if you have forgotten what transit friction feels like, travel somewhere without an open-loop option for vacation. Then, get frustrated and bring that feeling home. It’s the problem you’re solving for the visitor, tourist, and curious potential rider. The other modes travelers have available to them like scooters or rideshare, are nearly frictionless. Paying for transit must match this experience.

### Governance

Governance is a commonality across successful initiatives, especially for regional partnerships. Don’t assume that specifying the technology is enough for a partnership. You _must_ define roles because things will inevitably change: issues like who owns a decision, or how to break a stalemate, are the things that are going to break your forward motion, not technology specifications. Toronto learned this the hard way and their new regional modernization initiative is starting with establishing and documenting governance — no technical specifications needed.

### Agile, iterative, product mindsets

This year for the first time I heard these phrases almost as frequently as I’d heard them at software conferences in the 2010s. Agencies are starting to think like product owners, and it’s paying immediate dividends in meaningful and measurable outcomes.

### Simplify, simplify, simplify

This was a theme that not only carried across this conference agenda but was also a chorus I first heard earlier in 2026 from leaders at the California Passenger Rail Summit. Simplifying fare policies can both improve the quality of the rider experience and reduce technology costs.

## What should you do next?

### Talk to your riders

The last five years have been characterized by iterative testing and rollouts of open loop for riders in parallel to traditional closed-loop fare systems. For most agencies, marketing these technologies before they were stable was asking for bad press and angry riders. Instead, organic growth without marketing has enabled adoption of open-loop fare products between 10-20% for an agency after a year of operations.

Why this organic growth is so strong isn’t well known, and assumptions vary dramatically across geographies and populations. So, let’s ask riders, what brought you here? What keeps you here? For many agencies, the cost savings aren’t realizable without bigger adoption. Now is the time to tell your riders contactless is here, and that the potential impact for both you and them is enormous.

### Talk to your peers — frequently

Rapid adoption of interoperable off-the-shelf technologies means agencies can share lessons at a pace they haven’t been able to do before. Someone else is doing this right — maybe they already have a methodology, a draft policy.

Need help with debt recovery? Call ORCA.
Discounts for students on open loop? Talk to MST or Santa Cruz METRO.
Calculating cost of ownership? RTD said they’re moving forward with developing a "rigorous methodology.”
Need to educate and communicate with your riders about a new fare capping product? Toronto’s marketing videos are top notch.

We’re all peers, and we all love talking to transit. More importantly, we all want transit to succeed in every community. So make those email introductions — it can change everything.
